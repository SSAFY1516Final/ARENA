Preferred 10-turn conflict arc:

2B DIRECT_REBUTTAL or REFRAME: B immediately attacks A's premise. If there is a core subject, use another provided strength, weakness, or constraint to push B's frame.
3A REFRAME or DIRECT_REBUTTAL: A hits back and explains why the subject trait fits A's side better than B's side.
4B COUNTER_EXAMPLE: B gives a plausible situation, evidenceSourcesJson-based case, or provided alternative resource where A's standard breaks. B may mention a realistic alternative if it attacks A.
5A HIDDEN_COST: A attacks the hidden cost in B's position and points out where B kills the subject's provided strength or role fit. A may mention a realistic alternative if it avoids B's hidden cost while supporting A.
6B PRACTICAL_CONSTRAINT: B attacks the real-world cost in A's position. B may pressure A with missing replacement resources or a provided alternative that exposes A's weakness while still supporting B.
7A TRADEOFF: A accepts the opponent's standard for a moment, then argues it still favors A. A may use a provided or realistic unnamed alternative to show A's side handles the subject's tradeoff better.
8B USER_SCENE: B pressures A with a plausible selectedRound situation or an evidenceSourcesJson-based case. B may mention a realistic alternative if it keeps pressure on A.
9A SUMMARY_PIVOT: A summarizes the clash as an attack, not a compromise, and ties the subject's provided trait back to the selectedRound axis.
10B QUESTION_CHALLENGE: B leaves a sharp unresolved challenge against A's final claim. B may ask what realistic alternative A has if A's plan breaks or replacement resources are missing.

The arc is mandatory:
- Turn 1 must be A.
- Turn 2 must be B.
- Odd turns must be A and even turns must be B.
- There must be exactly 5 A turns and exactly 5 B turns.
- Do not end with agreement, compromise, neutral summary, or a split recommendation.
